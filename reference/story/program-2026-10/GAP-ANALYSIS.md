## 1. Executive summary (three minutes)

**Verdict.** This is an original, honest, fast and unusually well-engineered piece of marketing that argues the wrong product. It tells the Companion Hub story well (each person's job has an app that is an alternative to a rented service, told through fictional families, with the beta and fiction disclosures in the first screen) and it barely tells the Companion Core story: what the box is, why a box, local AI, memory, "arrives configured", and the one line the whole campaign hangs on, stop renting your intelligence. Around that, the buy path leaks attribution and is out of date, three required disclosures and several hard claims rules are broken on live pages, 27 of 30 families are pass-1 templates, and the "tool to make social videos" has produced no video.

Scores out of 5 (section 3): lander 2, tour 2.5, content 2, video 1.5, compliance 2.5, technical 3.5, funnel 1.5, product truth 2.5, home pages 2. The engineering floor is strong. The messaging and the plumbing around it are not.

**The five gaps that matter most**

1. **Neither the lander nor the tour says what a Companion Core is or makes the uncloud argument.** The H1 is "Thirty homes. Each family's life, on one Companion Core." The Core is named four times in the fold and defined zero times; the first definition is the last block on the page, 9.6 screens down on a desktop and 23.5 on a phone, in a 174 px text band. "Uncloud", "rent", "private", "2am" and "300+" appear zero times on the lander and zero to a handful of times in about 20,000 words of family copy. The tour spends 66.5 seconds (median, range 60 to 84) on unboxing before the first app-and-service pair. (W1-lander-01 to 05, W2-tour-04 to 06, W3-content-03, -08, -10)
2. **The conversion path is stale and leaking, and the campaign ends in 23 days.** `https://ci.computer/core` answers `301 location: http://ci.computer/store/p/core` and drops the whole query string (I re-ran the curl today; `/download` answers 200 with no redirect), landing on a priced store page ($5,900 for Core 128 against the $5,400 pledge in the campaign record) with no Kickstarter mention. The player never loads `campaign.js`, so the end card, About panel and header links leave the tour untagged. There is not one custom analytics event in the code, four unconnected GA properties, and Google Analytics runs with no consent step. (W7-funnel-01 to 04, -11, -14; W8-product-truth-06; W5-compliance-23)
3. **Live pages break the claims law and show things the product cannot do.** "Runs your own AI, apps and memory" (rule `cap-own-apps`, severity error) is on the lander, 30 home pages and the player, and `scripts/test-portal.mts:347` slices the lint window to stop before it. `launch-not-offer`, `not-frontier` and `beta-full` are required disclosures printed nowhere; the beta line is cut to "Companion OS is in beta." beside Memory and agents. Kiwix, which the catalog marks `avoid` because it ships an empty library, is shown answering look-ups in 15 families and is the hook of 16 of 89 planned ads. Children under 16 are shown on chat models in four more families, and a dead service (OurHome) is printed as what four families escaped. Set-up omits the CI Account and pairing step in all 30 tours. (W5-compliance-01 to 06; W8-product-truth-01, -02; W2-tour-01)
4. **27 of 30 families are pass-1 stories.** No `service` fields, 335 chips that say "replaces", 680 of 701 beats with no detail panel, nine to eleven apps over the 40 percent cap (the validator counts nine on the current tree) with 127 catalog apps never used, the same seven scaffold beats in every family, a gendered cast (organiser 23 of 24 women, gamer 7 of 7 men), Companion Memory in 5 of 30 families, no "leaving a service" scene anywhere. The family pages inherit the thinness. This is the expensive gap: the fix is a rewrite, not an edit. (W3-content-02, -03, -04, -11, -14, -18)
5. **The social-video tool has made nothing and a marketer cannot use it.** `video/` holds only `houses`; `films.json` is `{"families": {}}`; there are 0 voice locks, 0 films, 0 rendered cuts. The 89 planned cuts are one shape (one beat, one app, 15 to 18.7 s), the Core is on screen in 12, hooks are tour titles (two name the wrong person), the 9:16 safe zones are about half of what platform guides give, the end card prints 62 words in 3.5 seconds, and the only way to get a file is a CLI that needs a checkout, ffmpeg, Playwright and a GPU. (W4-video-01 to 05, -08, -11, -17, -18)

**What is strong and must be protected** (section 17 has the full list): the fictional-family plus app-and-service pair device (no competitor has it); "Fictional family. Real software." and the beta line in the first screen; zero dashes, exclamation marks, prices or performance numbers in shipped copy; 0 pair mismatches against the live 580-app store; the deterministic, random-access render path and the bitexact ad encode; static, no-JavaScript family cards and pages; the placement solver and type floor in the tour; `campaign.js`; the deploy token isolation; the first-run chapter as the only answer to the time objection.

**The recommendation in one paragraph.** Fix the claims and the buy path now (days, small token cost, Phase 1). Settle eight of the twenty owner decisions this week (D1, D2, D3, D4, D5, D8, D11, D15; section 15), because they gate most of the work. Restructure the lander, family page, home page and tour around one story, rent then move home then keep it (Phase 3). Rewrite the 27 families in tiers behind an "ad-ready" gate and send paid traffic only to families that pass it; do not spend on all 27 before the campaign closes (Phase 4). Then render, host and ship the first ad set and build the review and export surface (Phase 5). Phase 4 is where the tokens go.

## 2. The Companion Core story: what the approved pitch needs against what the experience says

The approved pitch (CI-Common `ADS.md` section 1) has four moves: open on a verb and a cost, name what it replaces before naming itself, carry the privacy argument with "your 2am questions", and answer the audience's real objection, which is the weekend self-hosting costs. `CAMPAIGN.md` adds the spine ("Every cloud-based AI you use is someone else's agent", "Core is the other option") and the sign-off "UnCloud your life."

| Moment | A stranger must understand | What the experience says today |
|---|---|---|
| 5 seconds (lander fold) | A personal server in the house holds your files and runs AI locally, so your life stops being rented. This is fiction. | "Thirty homes. Each family's life, on one Companion Core." Core named 4 times, defined 0 times. First line is a catalogue count. |
| 30 seconds (lander scroll) | Three proofs: each job has an alternative to a rented service; it is yours (local AI, memory, no API bill); it arrives configured. Beta. What to do. | Persona cards and 30 family cards. The only definition of the Core, "The small computer on your desk runs your own AI, apps and memory at home", is the last sentence before the footer, and it breaks `cap-own-apps`. |
| First minute of the tour | The stake, in a family's own words, with the pair on screen. | A parcel at the door. Unbox, plug in, join Wi-Fi, pick an agent, say hello. No app, no service, no cost until 66.5 s. |
| End of the tour | One sentence to repeat, what a Core is and is not, one next step. | "Your calendar, contracts, code and homework can sit on a Companion Core at home." Conditional ("can sit"), no recap, no Core in frame, destination is a priced store page. |
| End of an ad cut | The pitch line, the sign-off, what a Core is, one step. | A button reading "See Companion Core" and 62 words of disclosure. |

**Canon elements the experience omits or contradicts** (core-story section 3, product-truth-22): uncloud 0; "2am" 0; "double agents" 0; the weekend self-hosting costs 0; "configured" 0; "California" 0; "300+ apps and agents available in the Companion Hub store" 0; "no API bill" 0; the size claim (4.3 litre) 0. The allowed claims exist in `claims.json` (`problem`, `turn`, `core-size`, `core-quiet`, `core-assembled`, `hub-count`, `hub-no-api-bill`, `memory-file`, `plug-pair-walk`) and no public file uses them. Companion Memory, which the canon calls the reason to buy the hardware, is the beat's main app in 2 of 701 beats. Remote access, the pitch's "from anywhere", is barred by `cap-remote-access` while the live Kickstarter page already sells it (an owner ruling, D10).

**The one-sentence story (recommended).** A family stops renting its life from the cloud: its files, plans, games and questions move onto one quiet personal server in the house, the Companion Core, which arrives configured and runs the apps from the Companion Hub store.

**The arc, applied the same way on every surface.**
1. **Rent.** Every cloud AI is someone else's agent; name what this person rents today (the service, in a category noun in the first sentence).
2. **Move home.** The Core arrives configured (plug it in, pair it, walk away); each job gets an app that is an alternative to the service; the local model drafts, memory is a file on their disk.
3. **Keep it.** The same life now answers to them; the honest limit (capable open models, not the largest frontier model; beta); one next step.

**Headline candidates** (all checked against the `bannedPhrases` regexes by the core-story lens; copy must still pass `check-copy` before it ships): "Your AI should answer to you." (the canon's headline, verbatim); "Stop renting your intelligence." (pitch line one); "Stop renting your intelligence. Watch a family uncloud theirs." (lander-ux). My recommendation is the second as H1 with a one-sentence Core definition as the sub (D1), because it is the verb-and-cost opening the pitch requires and the click that arrives from the ad will have just read it.
